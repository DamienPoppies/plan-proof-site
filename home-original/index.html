Where the time actually goes

The job itself is not the problem. It is the six hand-offs around it — retyping, checking, remembering, and rebuilding the same document for the hundredth time.

01

The plan, photo or voice note

A PDF from the builder, a photo of a hand-drawn sketch, a message saying what the customer wants. Today it lands in your head and nowhere else.

02

What the job actually needs

Rooms, quantities, materials, access. Read off the document instead of retyped into a spreadsheet at nine at night.

03

What it cost you last time

Every item matched to your own past jobs — the rate you actually charge, the supplier price you actually paid — so the quote starts from evidence instead of memory.

04

The quote the customer reads

Your wording, your margin, a clean PDF in the customer's inbox before the competitor has opened their laptop.

05

The job pack

Order list, schedule and site notes, generated from the same job so nobody types it twice.

The gap

Why it is harder than it looks

01

You already know it is capable

You have used it. It wrote the awkward email, planned the trip, explained the thing you did not understand.

02

The demos do not help

Every example online is a demo. None of them tell you what to build for your Tuesday afternoon.

03

The gap is translation

Nobody has turned “we do this by hand” into “here is the tool, here is what it costs, here is what it replaces”.

That translation is the whole job. It is also the part most people skip.

The method

How it works

Three steps. You can stop after any of them, and you keep what you have paid for.

01

Name one job

60–90 minutes

We sit with the job you keep doing by hand. What it costs you now, what “done” looks like, and what you would never change.

You leave with the one-page plan: the job, the first version, and a price.

02

Build a working version

2–4 weeks

We build the smallest real version — your data, your words, your actual workflow. You click it while it is being built, not after.

You get a tool you can use on a real job, not a mockup of one.

03

Use it, then improve

Ongoing, small

Real use finds the real problems. We change what matters, leave what does not, and keep the whole thing documented.

You end up with something your team can run without us.

Boundaries

What we will not do

  • Sell you a strategy document If you want a slide about AI, this is not the place. The work is a tool you can use.
  • Pretend a demo is a product Anything we show you is either running on your data or clearly labelled as an illustration.
  • Put your customers somewhere you cannot control If a tool needs your data, that access stays with you and can be removed.
  • Make you depend on us If the honest answer is that you do not need this, that is the answer you will get.
