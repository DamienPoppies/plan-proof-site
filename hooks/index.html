Headline options

Three hooks, same page, same offer

Read each one as if you were a stranger who clicked an ad. The question is not which is cleverest — it is which makes the most people think “that is me” without promising something you cannot deliver.

A Current

For trade and service businesses

Your quotes go out at night. They shouldn’t have to.

Upload the plan, get the line items, send the quote — priced from the jobs you have already done. Book a free 30-minute call and we will find the job eating your week, then tell you in plain English what it would take to fix it.

Who it catches: someone who quotes for a living. Sharpest recognition of the three, and it pairs with the quoting demo. Who it loses: anyone whose problem is rostering, admin, stock or reporting — they read “quotes” and leave. Also signals trades only.

B Broader, keeps the job concrete

For owner-run businesses

The job you keep doing by hand is the one worth building.

Quoting, rostering, ordering, reporting — every business has one job that lands back on the owner’s desk. We find it, decide whether it is worth building, and tell you what it would cost. Free 30-minute call, one page back within 24 hours.

Who it catches: any owner who does something by hand every week — which is almost all of them. Keeps the word “hand”, so trades still feel seen. Who it loses: nobody much, but it asks for a small imaginative step: reading the headline and supplying their own job.

C Broadest

For businesses that have tried AI and shrugged

You know AI could do this. You just haven’t had time to work out how.

Most owners can see the gap. Very few have a spare fortnight to close it. Thirty minutes with us turns “we do this by hand” into a small working tool, a fixed price, and a plan you can act on.

Who it catches: the AI-curious of any industry, and the ones who have already tried ChatGPT and got nowhere. Widest reach. Who it loses: sceptics who read “AI” as hype, and anyone who wants to hear their own problem named rather than a category.

How to actually test it

Honest method, given no tracking

Do not A/B the same URL

The site sets no cookies and loads no analytics, so two headlines on one URL cannot be split or measured. Anything that claimed otherwise would be guessing.

Split by page, not by visitor

Put A on /lp/quotes/ and B on /. Run them as two separate ads to the same audience for ten days, and count booked calls — not clicks. Below roughly a hundred clicks per variant, treat the result as noise.

What to look for in the first week

Watch the booking rate and one qualitative signal: whether callers arrive already talking about a specific job. A headline that produces specific jobs is working even if the count is low.
