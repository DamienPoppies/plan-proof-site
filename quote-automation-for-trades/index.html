What quote automation actually means

It is not a quoting app you subscribe to. It is a small tool built around one job: taking a plan, a photo or a site note and turning it into line items priced the way your business already prices.

The work it removes is mechanical — reading quantities, looking up the last similar job, retyping the same eleven lines, chasing the version that went out on Tuesday. The judgement stays with you, which is why every quote a tool produces should arrive as a draft for a person to approve.

Where the hours go

Ask three trade business owners how long a quote takes and you will hear twenty minutes to two hours. Ask what happens at 9pm and you will hear the real answer.

The cost is rarely the writing. It is the search for the last price, the mental arithmetic on margin, the version control across email and phone, and the two days between the site visit and the number landing in the customer's inbox. A tool that closes that gap wins work against competitors who are still thinking about it.

Custom tool or quoting software

Buy software when your process is standard and the price is close to what the software assumes. Build a tool when your pricing has a shape only you know — a rate card with exceptions, a margin rule that depends on the job, a supplier arrangement nobody else has.

The honest answer is sometimes neither. If quotes are lost because nobody follows up rather than because they take too long, the tool to build is a follow-up system, not a quote builder. We work that out on the call before anything is scoped.

What a first version does, and does not

A first version reads one input type, prices from one source, and produces one editable draft quote. It does not integrate with your accounting system, run on a phone, or guess at quantities it cannot see.

That restraint is the point. A tool in daily use after three weeks beats a platform that is still being configured in month four.

This is worth a call if

  • You write five or more quotes a week, and they take longer than they should.
  • Your pricing lives in your head, a rate card, or the last nine jobs.
  • One person owns quoting decisions and can change how it happens.

It is not, if

  • You send two quotes a month and they are all bespoke.
  • Quotes are lost because nobody follows up, not because they take too long.
  • You want a mobile app or a full platform on day one.

Questions people actually ask

How much does a quote automation build cost?

A first version is quoted as a fixed price before work starts. The scoping fee comes off that price if you proceed.

Will it price jobs automatically without me checking?

No. It produces a draft with the reasoning visible, and a person approves every number before it reaches a customer.

Can it read our plans and drawings?

It can read a consistent input type — one drawing standard, one photo format. Mixed or hand-drawn inputs are scoped separately after we see them.

How long before it is in use?

Two to three weeks from signed scope for a first version, assuming one input type and one price source.

What if we already use quoting software?

Then the question is whether the gap is the software or the process around it. Sometimes the answer is to change the process, and we will say so.
