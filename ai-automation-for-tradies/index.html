AI automation

AI automation for tradies, minus the hype

You do not need an AI strategy. You need one job to stop eating your week. Here is what AI is genuinely good at in a trade business, and where it quietly fails.

What it is good at

Reading messy documents: plans, supplier invoices, handwritten site notes, emails from customers who write in paragraphs instead of bullet points.

Drafting the boring middle: a quote schedule, a follow-up message, a summary of what changed on a job. Anything where a first draft is most of the work and a person finishes it.

Matching one thing to another: this job looks like those nine jobs, so here is the rate and the likely extras.

What it is bad at

Being right without being checked. Anything that carries a price, a promise or a safety consequence needs a person to approve it, and any supplier telling you otherwise is selling.

Your pricing logic, if that logic lives in your head. It has to be written down once before it can be automated — which is often the most valuable hour in the whole project.

Anything customer-facing without review. Automated messages that guess wrong cost more than they save.

The jobs that pay for themselves

Quoting, because it happens weekly and the delay costs jobs. Follow-up, because enquiries go cold in days and nobody notices. Job paperwork, because it is done at night and gets done badly.

Rank them by hours per month and cost of mistakes. The top of that list is where a first tool should go — not the most exciting idea, the most repeated one.

What it costs in Australia

A first working tool is quoted as a fixed price at the end of scoping, and delivered in two to three weeks. That is deliberately small: one job, one input, one output, in daily use before anything else is added.

Anything quoted well below that is usually a template with your logo on it. Anything quoted far above it, for a business your size, is usually a platform you do not need yet.

This is worth a call if

  • Something in the business happens weekly, by hand, and only you know how.
  • You have tried a chat window and found it useful but not usable in the workflow.
  • You want one job fixed properly rather than a strategy document.

It is not, if

  • You are still deciding whether AI is real. That is a reading problem, not a build.
  • The process changes every month, so there is nothing stable to automate.
  • You want a guarantee of savings before starting.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need to understand AI to use this?

No. You need to understand your own job. The technical choices are ours to make and explain in plain English.

Is this just ChatGPT with extra steps?

Sometimes a chat window is enough, and we will say so rather than sell a build. A tool earns its cost when it runs the same job repeatedly, with your data and your rules.

Who owns the tool and the data?

You do, on final payment. Code, data and documentation are handed over; there is no licence and no retainer.

What about our customer data?

It stays in Australian accounts you control. The build team works against test data or through access you can revoke.

Can you guarantee it will save time?

No, and treat anyone who does with suspicion. The scope states what it will do and how we will both test that it does it.
