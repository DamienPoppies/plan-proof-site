Follow-up

Most quotes are not lost on price. They are lost on silence

A quote sent and never chased is a favour to your competitor. Follow-up automation is the least glamorous tool you can build, and usually the first one that pays.

The leak before the quote

Enquiries arrive by phone, text, form, Facebook message and word of mouth. Each one lands somewhere different, and the one that lands in a notification nobody saw is gone before it is ever quoted.

Then the quote goes out and the waiting begins. A week later you remember, feel awkward, and decide it is too late. It usually is not — it is just unrecoverable because nobody wrote down that it existed.

What the tool actually does

Captures every enquiry in one place, whatever channel it came from. Capturing is the unglamorous half that decides whether the rest works.

Runs a sequence you define: day two, day five, day twelve. Each message is drafted from the job details, and you approve the wording once rather than writing it each time.

Shows you what is outstanding, what has been chased, and what has gone quiet long enough to be worth a phone call rather than an email.

Why sequences beat reminders

A reminder depends on you having a free minute at the right moment. A sequence happens whether you are on a roof or at a funeral.

The other benefit is subtle: knowing the system will chase means you stop carrying the guilt of not chasing, which is the actual reason most follow-up does not happen.
