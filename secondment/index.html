Work How it works Seated with you About Start with one job

By arrangement · not the standard offer

Someone seated with you.

A person works inside your business for three to six months, following your lead rather than theirs. It is not a tool and it is not a project. It exists for the situation where the work is real but nobody on the team has the hours to do it.

Most of what this practice does is small and finite: one job, one tool, a fixed price, in weeks. This is the other shape of help, and it is deliberately not the default — because a person costs more than a tool and is much harder to undo.

It suits an owner who knows exactly what needs doing and does not want to explain it to a consultant. Someone sits alongside the team, takes direction from you, and spends their week on your list rather than on a statement of work.

Fit

When this makes sense

More work than hands

The list is longer than the team

Jobs are landing and the admin behind them is not keeping up, but there is no budget or appetite for a permanent hire yet.

Judgement, not a build

The work keeps changing shape

Too early to specify a system. What is needed is a person who can pick things up, do them properly, and tell you what should be built once the pattern is clear.

Tools nobody uses

You have bought things already

Software is in place and half of it is idle. Someone needs to sit with the team, learn the actual process, and make the tools stick.

How it runs

Your lead, their week.

Length

Three months to start, extendable to six by agreement. Both sides get a review point at the end of the first month, before anyone is committed to the rest.

Direction

You set the priorities, in writing, each week. They do not arrive with a transformation plan or a methodology to sell you.

What they do

The work you would do if you had the hours: quoting, follow-up, data cleanup, reporting, process write-ups, and the small tools that make those faster.

What they do not do

Your accounts, your hiring decisions, or anything a director should sign. They work inside your systems, under your authority, with access you can revoke the same day.

How it ends

With a written handover: what they did, what is left, and what should become a tool or a hire. Nothing should depend on them still being there.

What it costs

Agreed per engagement, monthly, before it starts. No published rate, because the shape of the arrangement changes the number.

Boundaries

What this is not

  • Not our core offer The work here is the small build. This sits beside it, for the situations that need hands rather than a tool.
  • Not labour hire or a replacement hire Different obligations, different paperwork. If a permanent role is what you need, hire for it directly.
  • Not a migration or visa arrangement Nothing here is a pathway to anything. Any arrangement is subject to the people being available and the terms being lawful and documented.
  • Not something to plan around Availability is limited and it is arranged case by case. It will usually be offered to people already working with us on a build.

Straight answers

Three questions worth asking

Is this cheaper than hiring?

For six months, sometimes. Over two years, almost never. A person who is staying learns your business; a person who is leaving takes that knowledge with them. Use this to cover a gap or prove a role, not to avoid a hire.

Do I have to have done a build first?

No, but it helps. A written scope from a build tells us what the work actually is, which makes the first month far more productive than starting from a conversation.

What happens to what they built while they were here?

It is yours, documented, with access you control. If something is worth turning into a proper tool, that becomes a normal project with a normal price.

If this is the shape you need

Ask on the call. It is one question.

The free call is still about one job. If the honest answer is a person rather than a tool, that is a short conversation, and it is worth having before anything is built.
