Build or buy

Custom AI tools for small business: when building beats buying

Software is cheap and plentiful. The expensive part is choosing wrong. This is the decision framework we use before anyone writes a line of code — including when the answer is to buy nothing.

The three questions

Does an existing product already run this job the way you run it? If yes, buy it and spend the money on the process around it.

Is the job repeated weekly, and does it involve documents, pricing or follow-up? That is where a small custom tool pays for itself fastest.

Can you describe the rule out loud? If the rule lives only in your head, the first deliverable is writing it down — which is what a scope is for.

Where custom wins

When your pricing, your paperwork or your job flow has a shape no product assumes: a rate card with exceptions, a margin that depends on the job type, a supplier arrangement unique to you.

When the tool has to sit beside what you already use rather than replace it — reading from the system you have, not migrating you to a new one.

When the volume is low but the value per job is high. A tool used eight times a week on jobs worth thousands does not need to be a platform.

Where custom loses

Bookkeeping, payroll, rostering and stock for a standard business. Products do this better and cheaper, and building it is a way to burn a quarter.

Anything that needs a mobile app on day one, integrations with five systems, or a guarantee of accuracy on numbers that matter commercially.

Anything you cannot describe to a stranger in two minutes. If the job resists description, it is not ready to build.

What the first version looks like

One input, one rule set, one output, one review round. A fixed price, two to three weeks, and the scoping fee credited against the build.

That buys a working tool your team uses, not a demonstration. If it does not pass the acceptance test written in the scope, fixing it is our problem.

This is worth a call if

  • You have a spreadsheet that has quietly become the business.
  • Off-the-shelf software almost fits, and the almost is costing you.
  • You can describe the rule out loud, even if nobody has written it down.

It is not, if

  • A standard product already does this job and you have not tried it.
  • The job happens once a quarter, however painful.
  • You need five integrations and a mobile app in the first release.

Questions people actually ask

How do we know if we need custom software?

Start with the free call. If a product already does the job, we will name it rather than build something.

What does a first build cost?

A fixed price agreed at the end of scoping, with the scoping fee credited against it. Larger systems are scoped separately and quoted before work starts.

Do you build mobile apps?

Not in a first version. A responsive web tool is faster to prove and cheaper to change.

What happens after launch?

You own the code and data. There is no retainer; changes are quoted as small fixed pieces of work, and you are free to take it elsewhere.

Can this integrate with our CRM or accounting?

Sometimes, and it is always scoped as separate, bounded work rather than assumed. Integrations are where fixed-price projects go to die.
