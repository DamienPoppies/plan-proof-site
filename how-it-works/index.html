How it works

One job, one small tool, no theatre.

Most of what goes wrong with business software is not technical. It is scope. So the work starts with a single job, builds the smallest useful version of it, and stops when it is working.

01

Name one job

30 minutes, no charge

One free session, thirty minutes, one job. We ask the questions that decide whether it is worth building: how often, how long, what it costs when it is wrong, and what would break if it disappeared.

You do not need to know anything about AI, tooling or software. You need to know the job.

You leave with one page: the job, the tool, the smallest version worth building, and a straight answer on whether to build it.

02

Build the smallest working version

2–4 weeks, fixed price

We build the version that does the job and nothing else. Your line items, your wording, your actual workflow. You see it running in the first week and use it before it is finished.

If something turns out to be harder than it looked, you hear it immediately — not at the end, in an invoice.

You get a tool you can use on a real job, run by real people, on your own data.

03

Use it, then improve it

Ongoing, small pieces

The first two weeks of real use are where the truth comes out. We fix what actually matters, delete what nobody touches, and write down what changed.

There is no retainer and no subscription. If it needs work, we quote it; if it does not, we say so.

You end up with something documented that your team can run without us.

The honest questions

Worth asking before you start

Do I need to understand AI to do this?

No. You need to understand the job. The useful knowledge sits with the person doing the work, not with the technology. If a tool would need you to learn something new just to operate it, it is the wrong tool.

What happens to my data?

It stays in accounts you own and can revoke. We work with the smallest amount of data that does the job — usually none of your customer records until the tool is running. Anything that needs access is named before it is connected.

Will this replace my current system?

Usually not. Most tools sit beside what you already use — your accounting package, your calendar, your inbox. Replacing a working system is a big decision and rarely the first thing worth doing.

What if it does not work?

Then you have lost a small, fixed amount on one job rather than a large amount on a platform. That is deliberate. The first conversation is free precisely so the answer can be “this is not worth building” without it costing you anything.

Who owns the tool?

You do. The code, the data and the accounts are yours. If you want to take it in-house or hand it to another firm, we help with the handover rather than making it awkward.

How big does my business need to be?

Big enough that the job happens every week, small enough that nobody has already solved it with a team and a system. Usually that means somewhere between two and thirty people.
