Work

Four tools, described plainly.

These are constructed examples, not client case studies. They exist to show the shape of the work: the job, the smallest useful tool, and what changes when people stop doing it by hand.

Example one · Trades

Quotes that take six minutes instead of forty

The job
A two-truck electrical business quoted from memory and a calculator. Prices drifted, margin was assumed, and quoting happened at night.
What was built
A quote builder with saved line items, a labour rate, a materials allowance and margin calculated on screen. Output is a clean PDF the customer can read.
What changed
Quotes go out the same day. The owner can see the margin before sending, instead of discovering it a month later.

Example two · Services

Every job has a status, a next step and a person

The job
A four-person crew tracked work in a group chat. Jobs were found by scrolling, and the same question was answered three times a day.
What was built
A simple job board: each job has a stage, a next action, a due date and one owner. Updates are two taps on a phone.
What changed
The morning question became “check the board”. Nothing is confirmed twice, and overdue jobs are visible rather than remembered.

Example three · Repeat business

The follow-up that used to depend on memory

The job
A service business sent quotes and then waited. Roughly a third were never chased, and past customers were never spoken to again.
What was built
A follow-up list that builds itself: quotes untouched for three days, jobs finished last month, customers due for a service. Each row has a drafted message the owner can edit.
What changed
Follow-up became a ten-minute ritual instead of an intention. Nothing is sent without a person reading it first.

Example four · Decisions

One page that answers “how did the week go?”

The job
An owner with three locations had numbers everywhere — two accounting exports, a roster tool, a paper diary — and no single view.
What was built
A weekly page with five figures that matter, each with last week and last month beside it. No dashboard theatre, no chart nobody reads.
What changed
Monday morning starts with the same five numbers. The question changed from “where is everything?” to “what do we do about that?”.

Being straight about this

Why there are no logos here

This practice is new. There is no portfolio of client work to point at, and inventing one would be the fastest way to lose the only thing that matters here — being believed.

So these examples are built, labelled and described honestly. When there is real work with a client’s permission, it will appear here with the same plainness: the job, the tool, what changed. No percentages that cannot be checked.
